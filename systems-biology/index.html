Stochasticity in Metabolism: Oxidative Stress Response Phenotypes As Emergent Consequences of Network Topology.

The authors extend the E. coli genome-scale metabolic model iJO1366 with 107 new reactions representing ROS chemistry (spontaneous oxidation, redox, and detoxification) and recast ROS dynamics as a flux-balance analysis optimization problem. This enables genome-scale simulation of oxidative stress response phenotypes, which they argue emerge from network topology and stochasticity rather than solely from kinetic parameters. The approach offers a scalable alternative to small ODE models that are limited by scarce kinetic data.

scPhyloGRN is a new computational method that infers gene regulatory networks from single-cell developmental transcriptomes by incorporating the lineage topology (cellular phylogeny) of the cells. This addresses a key challenge in single-cell GRN inference, where both cell state and lineage contribute to expression patterns.

The authors propose a per-edge arbitration approach for gene regulatory network inference, training a gradient-boosted classifier on the raw scores from ten different inference methods (including GENIE3, GRNBoost2, CLR, and ARACNe) so that method weighting varies edge-by-edge rather than using a single method or simple averaging.
