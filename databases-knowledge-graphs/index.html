HIT-MAP: A scalable approach to multimodal mapping of subcellular organization.

HIT-MAP is an end-to-end framework that combines endogenous epitope tagging, optimized wide-field immunofluorescence imaging, and affinity purification-mass spectrometry to map subcellular protein organization in a coordinated, scalable way. By avoiding target-specific antibodies and cDNA libraries, it lowers the cost and complexity barriers that previously restricted multimodal subcellular mapping to large consortia. The authors demonstrate the approach on a pilot set of 16 targets.

This preprint applies the ClinGen gene-disease validity framework to systematically classify the strength of evidence linking genes to primary immune regulatory disorders (PIRDs), a growing subset of inborn errors of immunity. The effort addresses a key bottleneck in the field: inconsistent and often limited evidence obscuring the genetic basis of these rare immune conditions.
