HIT-MAP: A scalable approach to multimodal mapping of subcellular organization.

HIT-MAP is an end-to-end framework that combines endogenous epitope tagging, optimized wide-field immunofluorescence imaging, and affinity purification-mass spectrometry to map subcellular protein organization in a scalable, cost-effective way. By avoiding target-specific antibodies and cDNA libraries, it lowers barriers for labs beyond large consortia to perform multimodal subcellular mapping. The pilot application to 16 targets demonstrates feasibility of coordinated imaging and interaction proteomics.

Highly contiguous reference genome assembly of the endangered Orcés' blue whiptail Holcosus orcesi.

This paper presents the first high-quality reference genome for Holcosus orcesi, a Critically Endangered lizard endemic to a small region in southern Ecuador. Using Oxford Nanopore long-read sequencing, the authors produced a highly contiguous 1.68 Gb assembly with an N50 of 76.2 Mb and 96.8% BUSCO completeness, making it one of the most contiguous squamate genomes to date. This resource will support conservation genomics and evolutionary studies of this rare montane lineage.
