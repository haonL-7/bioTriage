EchoGO: annotation-context-aware functional interpretation of transcriptomic data.

EchoGO is a reproducible framework for functional enrichment of transcriptomic data that runs GOseq enrichment while comparing results across multiple organism annotation contexts using experiment-specific backgrounds. It assembles evidence at the GO-term level with analytical and organism provenance, helping address incomplete annotation and uneven identifier recognition. The tool is aimed at making downstream functional interpretation more transparent and reproducible.

Cutibacterium acnes induces T-cell extracellular traps with distinct proteome programs.

This study shows that both acne-associated and healthy skin-associated strains of Cutibacterium acnes trigger rapid extracellular trap release from human neutrophils and Th17 cells. While neutrophil extracellular traps had a conserved proteomic profile regardless of strain, T-cell extracellular traps (TETs) exhibited distinct proteome programs depending on the C. acnes strain. The work links a skin commensal to adaptive immune cell trap formation and suggests strain-specific functional consequences relevant to acne pathogenesis.

Phenologs at knowledge-graph scale: a mouse-to-slime-mold catalog of candidate genes for thousands of human diseases.

This paper computes phenologs—pairs of human diseases and model-organism phenotypes whose associated genes overlap more than expected by chance after orthology mapping—at knowledge-graph scale using the Monarch Knowledge Graph covering human and seven model species. The result is a catalog of candidate human disease genes derived from mouse-to-slime-mold phenotype annotations, extending phenolog-based gene discovery to thousands of diseases.
