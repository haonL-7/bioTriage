HIT-MAP: A scalable approach to multimodal mapping of subcellular organization.

HIT-MAP is an end-to-end framework that combines endogenous epitope tagging, optimized wide-field immunofluorescence imaging, and affinity purification-mass spectrometry to map subcellular protein organization in a coordinated, scalable way. By avoiding reliance on target-specific antibodies or cDNA libraries, it lowers the cost and complexity of multimodal subcellular mapping, making it accessible beyond large consortia. The authors demonstrate the approach on a pilot set of 16 targets.

PDBe-KB Complexes: Enabling Functional Insight from Macromolecular Complexes in the PDB.

PDBe-KB Complexes is a new resource that groups equivalent macromolecular assemblies from the PDB into unified complex-level records, consolidating data on component identity, stoichiometry, symmetry, ligands, and PISA-derived assembly properties. It also links related complexes by comparing their compositions to capture subcomplex relationships, providing functional context for structural assemblies.
