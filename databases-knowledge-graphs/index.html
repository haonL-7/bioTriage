InterPro: the protein sequence classification resource in 2027.

InterPro is a freely accessible database that integrates predictive models (signatures) from multiple member databases to classify protein sequences into families and predict domains and functional sites. The 2027 update provides annotations for hundreds of millions of sequences, achieving extensive coverage of UniProtKB and linking to resources like Gene Ontology and PDBe. This makes it a central hub for protein functional annotation.

Cutibacterium acnes induces T-cell extracellular traps with distinct proteome programs.

This study shows that both acne-associated and healthy skin-associated strains of Cutibacterium acnes trigger rapid extracellular trap release from human neutrophils and Th17 cells. While neutrophil extracellular traps had a conserved proteomic profile regardless of strain, T-cell extracellular traps induced by the two strains showed distinct proteomic programs, suggesting strain-specific functional consequences in acne pathogenesis.

Integration of genotyping by sequencing and machine learning for genome-wide identification of informative markers for body weight in broiler chickens.

This study used a cost-effective genotyping-by-sequencing (GBS) approach combined with Random Forest and Gradient Boosting Machine learning to identify SNP markers associated with body weight in broiler chickens. The work demonstrates that GBS plus ensemble ML can substitute for expensive high-density SNP arrays in genome-wide association studies for poultry breeding, identifying informative markers for body weight at hatch and 3 weeks.

Phenologs at knowledge-graph scale: a mouse-to-slime-mold catalog of candidate genes for thousands of human diseases.

The authors compute phenologs—statistically significant overlaps between human disease genes and model-organism phenotype genes linked by orthology—at knowledge-graph scale using the Monarch Knowledge Graph across human and seven model species. Using PANTHER protein families as orthology units, they produce a mouse-to-slime-mold catalog of candidate genes for thousands of human diseases, demonstrating that even distant model organisms can nominate disease gene candidates.
