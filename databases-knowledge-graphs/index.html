HIT-MAP: A scalable approach to multimodal mapping of subcellular organization.

HIT-MAP is an end-to-end framework that combines endogenous epitope tagging, optimized wide-field immunofluorescence imaging, and affinity purification-mass spectrometry to map protein subcellular localization and interactions in a coordinated, scalable way. It aims to make multimodal subcellular mapping accessible beyond large consortia by reducing per-target costs and eliminating reliance on target-specific antibodies or cDNA libraries. The approach was piloted on 16 targets, demonstrating a unified pipeline for integrated imaging and interaction proteomics.

EBV-HSA-RegDB: A database of regulatory interactions between Epstein-Barr virus and human miRNAs.

EBV-HSA-RegDB is a new database that catalogs regulatory interactions between Epstein-Barr virus-encoded miRNAs and human miRNAs, built by integrating comparative sequence analysis and other bioinformatics approaches. It aims to help researchers understand how EBV miRNAs and host miRNAs influence each other during infection, which is relevant to EBV-associated cancers and autoimmune diseases. The resource is freely accessible online and provides a starting point for studying viral-host miRNA regulatory networks.

This perspective reviews how spatial omics technologies are being applied to plant biology, moving beyond spatial transcriptomics toward integrated multi-omics that combines single-cell and spatial transcriptomic, epigenomic, proteomic, and metabolomic data.
