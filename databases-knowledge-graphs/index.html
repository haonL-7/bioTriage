Phenologs at knowledge-graph scale: a mouse-to-slime-mold catalog of candidate genes for thousands of human diseases.

The authors compute phenologs—pairs of human diseases and model-organism phenotypes whose associated genes overlap more than expected by chance after orthology mapping—at knowledge-graph scale using the Monarch Knowledge Graph, covering human and seven model species. Using PANTHER protein families as orthology groups, they produce a catalog of candidate human disease genes derived from mouse-to-slime-mold phenotype annotations for thousands of diseases. This provides a systematic, principled resource for cross-species gene discovery in human disease.

Cutibacterium acnes induces T-cell extracellular traps with distinct proteome programs.

This study shows that both acne-associated and healthy skin-associated strains of the skin bacterium Cutibacterium acnes trigger neutrophils and Th17 T cells to release extracellular traps (web-like DNA structures). While neutrophil traps looked the same regardless of bacterial strain, T-cell traps induced by the two strains had distinct proteomic programs, suggesting strain-specific functional consequences. This links a common skin commensal to immune cell trap formation with potential relevance to acne pathogenesis.
