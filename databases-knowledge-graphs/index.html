Defining the Genetic and Phenotypic Landscape of Primary Immune Regulatory Disorders Using the ClinGen Validation Framework.

This preprint applies the ClinGen gene-disease validity framework to systematically classify the strength of evidence linking genes to primary immune regulatory disorders (PIRDs), a growing subset of inborn errors of immunity. By standardizing how published data are evaluated, the work aims to clarify which gene-disease relationships are well-supported versus those obscured by limited or conflicting evidence. The result is a curated landscape of genetic and phenotypic features that can guide clinical interpretation and future research in immune dysregulation.

This paper introduces iMPI, a database cataloging microproteins that could be produced from retained introns in tumor cells. The authors systematically searched retained introns for protein-coding open reading frames, addressing a gap in resources linking intron retention to potential cancer-relevant proteins. Functional mapping of the Trypanosoma cruzi serinome by fluorophosphonate activity-based protein profiling.

This study maps the active serine hydrolase enzymes (the 'serinome') of Trypanosoma cruzi, the parasite that causes Chagas disease, using activity-based protein profiling with fluorophosphonate probes combined with quantitative proteomics. The authors identified 37 enriched serine hydrolase-like proteins in the epimastigote stage, providing a functional annotation resource for this largely uncharacterized enzyme family. This chemoproteomic map lays groundwork for targeting these enzymes in Chagas disease drug development.

PloS one 2026--01 Medium

Highly contiguous reference genome assembly of the endangered Orcés' blue whiptail Holcosus orcesi.

This paper presents the first high-quality reference genome for Holcosus orcesi, a Critically Endangered lizard endemic to southern Ecuador, assembled using Oxford Nanopore long-read sequencing. The 1.68 Gb assembly is highly contiguous (91 contigs, N50 of 76.2 Mb) and complete (96.8% BUSCO), making it one of the most contiguous squamate genomes to date. This resource supports conservation genomics and evolutionary studies of this rare montane lineage.
