HIT-MAP: A scalable approach to multimodal mapping of subcellular organization.

HIT-MAP is an end-to-end framework that combines endogenous epitope tagging, optimized wide-field immunofluorescence imaging, and affinity purification-mass spectrometry to map subcellular protein organization in a coordinated, scalable way. By avoiding target-specific antibodies and cDNA libraries, it lowers the cost and complexity that previously restricted multimodal subcellular mapping to large consortia. The authors demonstrate the approach on a pilot set of 16 targets.

PDBe-KB Complexes: Enabling Functional Insight from Macromolecular Complexes in the PDB.

PDBe-KB Complexes is a new resource that aggregates experimentally determined macromolecular assemblies from the PDB into unique complex-level records, unifying equivalent biological assemblies and consolidating information on components, stoichiometry, symmetry, ligands, and assembly properties. It also captures relationships between complexes by comparing their compositions, including subcomplex relationships, enabling functional insight at the level of whole complexes rather than individual structures.

Rabies Virus Glycoprotein G Variation Is Associated with Neurovirulence and Reduced Vamp2 Abundance in a Mouse Model of Direct CNS Infection.

The study generated two recombinant rabies viruses differing only in their glycoprotein G (from attenuated SAD-B19 or virulent CVS11 strains) and compared their effects in mouse neurons and mice. The virulent G variant increased early neuronal infectivity and neurovirulence after direct CNS injection, and was linked to reduced abundance of the synaptic protein Vamp2. This suggests glycoprotein G variation contributes to rabies neurovirulence partly through disruption of synaptic vesicle machinery.
