HIT-MAP: A scalable approach to multimodal mapping of subcellular organization.

HIT-MAP is an end-to-end framework that combines endogenous epitope tagging, optimized wide-field immunofluorescence imaging, and affinity purification-mass spectrometry to map subcellular protein organization in a coordinated, scalable way. By avoiding the need for target-specific antibodies or cDNA libraries, it lowers the cost and complexity of multimodal subcellular mapping, making it feasible beyond large consortia. The authors demonstrate the approach on a pilot set of 16 targets.

PDBe-KB Complexes: Enabling Functional Insight from Macromolecular Complexes in the PDB.

PDBe-KB Complexes is a new resource that groups equivalent experimentally determined macromolecular assemblies from the PDB into unified complex-level records, consolidating information on component identity, stoichiometry, symmetry, ligands, and PISA-derived assembly properties. It also captures relationships between complexes by comparing their compositions, including subcomplex relationships, enabling functional insight at the level of biological assemblies rather than individual structures.

Highly contiguous reference genome assembly of the endangered Orcés' blue whiptail Holcosus orcesi.

This paper presents the first high-quality reference genome assembly for Holcosus orcesi, a Critically Endangered lizard endemic to a small region in southern Ecuador. Using Oxford Nanopore long-read sequencing, the authors produced a highly contiguous 1.68 Gb assembly with an N50 of 76.2 Mb and 96.8% BUSCO completeness, making it one of the most contiguous squamate genomes to date. This resource will support conservation genomics and evolutionary studies of this rare montane lineage.
