From saccharides to methane: substrate-driven microbial diversity and methanogenesis in anaerobic digestion revealed by 390 MAGs.

This study used genome-centric metagenomics to reconstruct 390 high-quality metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs) from synthetic anaerobic digestion systems fed with five different substrates (starch, glucose, butyrate, propionate-valerate, and acetate). Nearly two-thirds of the MAGs represent potentially novel species, and the authors show that substrate type drives selection of distinct functional microbial consortia, with specific taxa enriched for particular degradation steps (e.g., Bifidobacterium in starch hydrolysis). The work provides a large genome resource and links substrate chemistry to community structure and methanogenesis pathways.

Metabolomics in the wild: A scoping review of non-invasive methods for understanding vertebrate wildlife health and success.

This scoping review maps the use of non-invasive metabolomics sampling methods in wild vertebrate research, finding that the field is still in its early stages and has primarily focused on humans, domesticated, and model species rather than wildlife.
