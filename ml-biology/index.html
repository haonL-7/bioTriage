This study investigates why the distribution of fitness effects (DFE) of new mutations varies across the genome by inferring gene-level selective constraints in mice, fruit flies, and yeast using population genetics and machine learning. The authors find that many diverse gene features predict selective constraint, and that gene-level complexity—likely reflecting the number and nature of functional constraints—explains a substantial portion of genome-wide variation in the DFE. This provides a unifying framework linking genomic features to the evolutionary consequences of new mutations.
