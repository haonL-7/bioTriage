Frustration Quenching and Network Topology of the Energy Landscape as Primary Determinants of Protein-Ligand Binding Pose Prediction by Deep Learning Models.

This preprint shows that deep learning models for protein-ligand pose prediction perform worse at allosteric pockets than orthosteric ones because of a biophysical property of the binding site, not an algorithmic flaw. Specifically, prediction success tracks the magnitude of 'frustration quenching'—the reduction in local energetic frustration when a ligand binds—and the topology of the energy landscape. The authors argue that frustration quenching is a primary determinant of whether AI co-folding and docking models succeed.
