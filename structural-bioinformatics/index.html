Structure-based minimization of a high-mobility group protein B1 (HMGB1) B box peptide uncouples anti-NETotic activity and anti-biofilm function.

This study shows that the anti-biofilm and anti-NETosis activities of an HMGB1 B-box-derived peptide can be separated into distinct shorter peptide variants. Truncated peptides containing Helix 1 of the B-box retained one function while losing the other, demonstrating that these two activities are structurally uncoupled. This provides a basis for engineering HMGB1 peptides with selective therapeutic functions.

Unleashing the industrial potential of microbial plant cell wall polysaccharide-degrading enzymes: recent advances in protein engineering.

This review surveys recent protein engineering advances aimed at improving microbial plant cell wall polysaccharide-degrading enzymes (cellulases, LPMOs, xylanases, mannanases, pectinases) for industrial use. It highlights how structure- and sequence-guided engineering strategies address limitations such as low catalytic efficiency, poor thermostability, and product inhibition across biorefinery, food/feed, textile, and pulp applications.
