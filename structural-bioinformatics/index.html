Biophysically grounded deep learning improves protein-protein ΔΔG prediction.

The paper shows that standard SKEMPI2 train-test splits overestimate the accuracy of AI models for predicting mutation-induced changes in protein-protein binding affinity (ΔΔG) due to homology leakage. The authors introduce ProtBFF, a modular framework that combines five residue-level biophysical descriptors with pretrained protein representations via cross-embedding attention, and demonstrate improved performance under a stringent homology-controlled split (60% sequence identity at the chain level).

Specifics of Classical Molecular Dynamics Simulation of the Raman Spectrum of the CO2 Fermi Dyad.

This study shows that discrepancies in simulated Raman Fermi dyad positions for supercritical CO2 stem mainly from how the simulation is prepared rather than from the force field used. A global Nosé-Hoover thermostat leaves the vibrational subsystem 40-100 K below the target temperature for hundreds of picoseconds, shifting the dyad splitting by up to 20 cm-1 and the NPT density by 50-70%, while a per-atom Langevin thermostat eliminates both errors.

GPCRVS in the detection of target-selective molecular scaffolds based on deep learning and gradient boosting.

GPCRVS is a web service that combines deep learning and gradient boosting to virtually screen compounds for selective binding to G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs), a challenging drug target family due to their structural similarity. The tool aims to help identify drug-like molecules that preferentially hit one GPCR over others, reducing off-target effects. It provides a practical resource for computational drug discovery focused on GPCR selectivity.
