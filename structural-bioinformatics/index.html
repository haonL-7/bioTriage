Scaffold-Constrained Generative Design Overcomes Failure Modes in Computational Protein Engineering.

The authors identify systematic failure modes that arise when unconstrained generative protein models are applied to fixed structural scaffolds, such as glycine enrichment and grammar violations. They introduce DARPinMPNN, a scaffold-constrained framework that restricts sequence generation to permitted DARPin Greek Grammar residue pools, and use it to design 15,000 variants targeting mesothelin, VEGF-A, and other targets, screened with AlphaFold2-Multimer and AlphaFold3. The approach improves the reliability of computational binder design by enforcing scaffold-compatible sequence constraints.

What deep learning can and cannot (yet) do for enzyme design.

This review examines the current capabilities and limitations of deep learning for enzyme design, highlighting that while diffusion-based scaffold generation combined with sequence design has expanded accessible reaction space and produced enzymes with diverse catalytic functions, reliably achieving high catalytic efficiency remains elusive. Notably, while catalytic efficiency (kcat/KM) can be impressive, turnover rates (kcat) often remain limited, indicating that current methods typically yield enzymes with tight substrate binding but suboptimal product release.
