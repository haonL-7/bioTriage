Scaffold-Constrained Generative Design Overcomes Failure Modes in Computational Protein Engineering.

The authors identify systematic failure modes that occur when unconstrained generative protein models are applied to fixed structural scaffolds, such as glycine enrichment and grammar violations. They develop DARPinMPNN, a scaffold-constrained framework that restricts sequence generation to permitted DARPin Greek Grammar residue pools, and use it to design 15,000 variants targeting mesothelin and VEGF-A, screened with AlphaFold2-Multimer and AlphaFold3. This approach overcomes the artifacts seen in unconstrained design and improves the reliability of computational protein engineering.

What deep learning can and cannot (yet) do for enzyme design.

This review examines the current capabilities and limitations of deep learning for enzyme design, highlighting that while diffusion-based scaffold generation combined with sequence design has expanded accessible reaction space and produced enzymes with diverse catalytic functions, reliably achieving high catalytic efficiency remains elusive. The key bottleneck is that while catalytic efficiencies (kcat/KM) can be notable, turnover rates (kcat) often remain limited, indicating that current approaches typically yield tight substrate binding but poor product release.
