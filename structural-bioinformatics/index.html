This review examines how protein engineering is being used to improve microbial plant cell wall polysaccharide-degrading enzymes (such as cellulases, LPMOs, xylanases, mannanases, and pectinases) for industrial applications. It highlights the key limitations of these enzymes—low catalytic efficiency, poor thermostability, limited operational adaptability, and product inhibition—and surveys structure- and sequence-guided engineering strategies to overcome them across biorefinery, food/feed, textile, and pulp industries.

Tailoring plant-based food emulsions with oil bodies and proteins recovered by alkali aqueous extraction from rapeseed (Brassica napus L.) oilseeds.

This study demonstrates that oil bodies and storage proteins can be co-extracted from rapeseed oilseeds using alkaline aqueous extraction at pH 9.5, yielding well-dispersed oil bodies suitable for plant-based food emulsions. The authors characterize the microstructure and composition of the extracts and evaluate how pH affects the physical stability of natural oil bodies, homogenized oil bodies, proteins, and protein-stabilized oil droplets in emulsions. The work provides practical insight into using these co-extracted ingredients to tailor emulsion stability for plant-based food applications.

Exploring molecular interactions between yeast topoisomerase I and RNA polymerase III subunit, Rpc82 using an in-silico approach.

This study uses computational docking and molecular dynamics to model how yeast topoisomerase I (Top I) interacts with Rpc82, a subunit of RNA polymerase III. The authors identify putative interaction interfaces and structural domains that may mediate this binding, offering hypotheses about how Top I is recruited to relieve transcription-associated torsional stress. The work is purely in-silico and provides a structural framework for future experimental validation.
