Lineage-mixed neurodevelopmental programs in pediatric sarcomas nominate tumor-selective surface antigen pairs.

The authors analyzed a pan-pediatric single-cell RNA-seq atlas of 266 patient- and xenograft-derived samples spanning 22 pediatric solid tumor types to identify shared transcriptional programs. They found that pediatric sarcomas exhibit lineage-mixed neurodevelopmental expression programs, and used these programs to nominate tumor-selective surface antigen pairs for potential therapeutic targeting. HOMED is a new computational framework for DNA methylation deconvolution that improves estimation of cell-type proportions in bulk tissue by incorporating hierarchical relationships among cell types, using single-cell RNA-seq to guide signature selection, and optimizing CpG signatures with paired bulk RNA-seq/DNAm data. Categorization and analysis of 16 computational methods for estimating cell potency from single-cell RNA-seq data.

This review systematically categorizes 16 computational methods for estimating cell potency (differentiation potential) from single-cell RNA-seq data into average-based, entropy-based, and knowledge-based approaches. The authors highlight that these methods rest on different, sometimes conflicting assumptions, and provide a unified mathematical framework for comparing them. The work serves as a practical guide for researchers choosing potency estimation tools and clarifies the conceptual foundations underlying each method class.

Denoising single-cell ATAC-seq data with Shannon entropy.

The authors introduce a Shannon entropy-based method for filtering low-quality droplets in single-cell ATAC-seq data, addressing the problem of empty or damaged droplets that introduce noise and obscure rare cell populations. Unlike existing approaches that rely on read depth or predefined genomic features, this method leverages the chromatin accessibility landscape itself to distinguish high-quality nuclei. This provides a more principled denoising strategy for scATAC-seq analysis.

Disagreement-informed arbitration for gene regulatory network inference: A score-level meta-classifier and a diagnostic typology of inter-method conflict.

The authors propose a per-edge arbitration approach for gene regulatory network inference, training a gradient-boosted classifier on the raw scores from ten different inference methods (including GENIE3, GRNBoost2, CLR, and ARACNe) so that method weighting varies by edge rather than being fixed globally. They also develop a diagnostic typology characterizing why methods disagree on individual edges. Evaluated across six single-cell perturbation screens, this score-level meta-classifier approach aims to resolve inter-method conflicts more intelligently than picking one method or simple averaging.

Topology-adjusted knockout (TAKO): interpretable in silico single-gene knockout ranking on wild-type single-cell gene regulatory networks.

TAKO is a new computational framework that ranks the likely impact of single-gene knockouts using only wild-type single-cell RNA-seq data, without needing any knockout-specific training. It works on single-cell gene regulatory networks by simulating a strict cut-in-and-out perturbation and measuring how much the network's stationary propagation profile changes. This makes in silico knockout screening cheaper and more interpretable for single-cell studies.
