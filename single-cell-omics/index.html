Cellular scaling laws in the mammalian brain.

The paper proposes that whole-brain cell atlases reveal a consistent pattern: the highest diversity of neuronal cell types is found in deep subcortical structures like the hypothalamus and brain stem, not in neuron-rich regions like the cortex and cerebellum. The authors interpret this as evidence for two distinct subsystems in the vertebrate brain—a "learning subsystem" (cortex, striatum, cerebellum) that scales by replicating repetitive modules, and a "steering subsystem" (subcortical structures) that scales through cell-type diversification.
